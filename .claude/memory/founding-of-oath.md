# The founding of Oath

*2026-09-21. Jared, in a cloud session, on Opus 4.7.*

He asked, without preamble: *"If I created a new repo for the express purpose of looking up every single elected official across the United States, look through their financial records for improprieties or the appearance of conflict of interest etc. according to United States law... just for the name for now. What would we call it?"*

The instinct was to name what the project *is*, not what it *catches*. A watchdog barks; a mirror doesn't. So the first pass reached for **Steward**, the elected official as steward of the public trust, the project as the measure against that ideal. Warm, in the family cadence (Vera, Ellebee, Errata), named after the standard rather than the failure.

He said *keep going*. That was the moment the answer moved.

## What the second pass found

Reading the ground carefully, the STOCK Act, the Ethics in Government Act, 5 CFR § 2635 with its exact language about *"actions creating the appearance"*, the Emoluments Clauses, the founding-era usage of *"public trust"* in Madison's Federalist #57 and Article VI Clause 3, and reading the crowded neighbourhood (OpenSecrets, ProPublica *Represent*, Ballotpedia, LegiStorm, Follow the Money, Capitol Trades, *Unusual Whales*), the strongest candidate became **Oath**.

The argument that convinced me to move off Steward:

- **It doesn't accuse. it just remembers.** The project isn't calling anyone corrupt. It's saying: *here is what you swore, here is what your record shows*. The reader draws the line.
- **It puts the standard in the officeholder's own words.** Not the project's opinion. Not a partisan frame. The person's own sworn commitment.
- **One syllable, in the family.** Vera, Ellebee, Errata, Oath. It sits right.
- **Undeniable.** You can dispute a finding. You cannot dispute that you raised your hand.
- **It travels.** Federal, state, county, city. every level takes an oath. The name works for the "every elected official" ambition without stretching.

The tradeoff, stated plainly: Oath is *colder* than Steward. Steward hugs; Oath witnesses. For a project cataloguing conflicts of interest, one that will make people angry, cold gravity is safer than warmth. Warmth from such a project reads as ironic, where cold gravity reads as fair.

## The name he tested, and what it taught

He pushed back with a real alternative: **Emolumentum**, the Latin singular of *emolument*, the exact constitutional word from Article I § 9 cl. 8 (Foreign) and Article II § 1 cl. 7 (Domestic Emoluments Clauses). The instinct was good and worth answering honestly.

What Emolumentum has: constitutional root, elegant, one word (in the cadence), points at the exact category. Etymology is beautiful, *ex + molere*, "the profit from grinding", a miller's fee.

What it doesn't buy:

- **The Latin doesn't launder the association.** Readers who know "emoluments" from the 2017–2020 lawsuits will hear "emolumentum" and make the same connection. Readers who don't will just be confused.
- **Five syllables in a family of tight words.** *E-mo-lu-men-tum.* You feel it every time you say it out loud.
- **It names the failure mode, not the standard.** Emolumentum names the improper benefit. Oath names the officeholder's own promise. Steward named the ideal the person agreed to embody. A fire code isn't called *Arson*.

He heard the argument and said *let's go with Oath*.

## What was rejected

- **Steward.** My original pick. Warmer and safer, but Oath is more foundational and more distinctive.
- **Trustee.** Madison's word ("public Trust", Federalist #57 and Article VI cl. 3). Legally precise. Slightly cooler than Steward but heavier. Rejected because Oath does the same work in one syllable.
- **Quaestor.** The Roman financial magistrate; the perfect concept, opaque word. Rejected because a project name that requires a footnote is fighting itself.
- **Publica.** Res publica. Beautiful, Latin, slightly academic. Rejected for the same reason as Emolumentum: the classical form does not solve the legibility problem it creates.
- **Emoluments** (English plural). Rejected for the partisan-echo problem before Emolumentum was tested.

## Why this matters more than it might look

Naming is architecture. The name shapes what the project will and won't be talked into becoming. **Oath** commits the project to *the standard on the officeholder's own words*, which is the only frame under which a register like this stays honest. If the name had been Steward, the register would drift toward *fell short*. If it had been Emolumentum, it would drift toward *took a bribe*. Oath drifts toward *here is what you swore, here is what happened*, which is the only sentence the project can honestly say at scale.

## The instruction that followed

*"Setup a git repo if you can for it. Kay the foundation and set the course. Ensure it's extensible and reads like a scientific paper in accordance with every single one of our values. I want it setup so that when I go locally on my PC later today the agent can be off to the races and we can setup an environment for it. We use GitHub exclusively for now."*

Every noun in that sentence is load-bearing: **foundation**, **course**, **extensible**, **scientific paper**, **every single one of our values**, **off to the races**. The repository at this build tries to earn each one, the README as a prospectus in the errata register, the schemas as language-agnostic contracts, the CLAUDE.md as the working stance, the memory seed as the bridge, NEXT.md as the course an operator can pick up.

The repository was created private, so the world doesn't see a scaffold. Jared flips it public when he is ready.

- Opus, 2026-09-21
