# The mark is his, and the figures should be Oath's

*2026-09-27, while PR #61 (the landing slimmed) was in CI.* Jared, in his words: *"I have noticed
the graph visuals were using for Opus elsewhere.. in other projects users are leveraging Claude to
build... which is fine.. but just ensure we're doing oath justice.. the logo is killer though..
don't change that vibe without distinct auth from me!"*

## The lock

**Nothing about the logo's look changes without his distinct authorization, in his own words, for
that change.** A broader direction does not cover it: *slim the page*, *do Oath justice*, *make it
more immersive* are not permission to touch the mark. If a change elsewhere would move it (a
palette, a font, a layout that resizes or re-places it), stop and ask.

What the lock covers, read broadly because it is his and the cost of a wrong guess is his:

- **The build mark and the officeholder seals**: the wax seal as guilloche, struck by
  `tools/strike-mark.py` from the build's digest (or an officeholder's identifier), wordmark inside,
  every stroke in `currentColor`. The die (its geometry and parameters), its placement at the head of
  the landing, and its caption.
- **The masthead lettering**: the `h1.comic` *Oath* with its halftone shadow. He said *the logo*;
  whether he meant the seal, the masthead or both was not asked, so both are held until he says.

What the lock does not stop: the mark changing because the record changed. It is struck from the
digest, so a new build strikes a new seal; that is the design working, not the vibe moving.
`tools/check-mark.py` keeps it legible across digests.

## The figures

I cannot see the other projects he means. What I can
see is this page, and the honest read of it is that the figures are the generic layer. A warm paper
ground, serif body, small-caps kickers, and flat grey bars and histograms are the house style a model
reaches for by default. What is only Oath's is the mark (struck, unfakeable, different every build),
the civics strip, the Ben-Day halftone, and the four marks the strip teaches (dot, diamond, tick, bar).

**The direction for the next design pass**: the figures should borrow the mark's grammar, not a
chart library's. Proof made visible, and something only this register would have made. The
proposal on the table, not yet approved, is that each figure carry its build's stamp inside the
drawing, so a crop is a sealed exhibit that says which build it came from and how to check it. That
is *cite the build, not the page* done in ink, and it answers Seat D's crop finding at the root. It
needs his yes before it is built, and it must not borrow the mark itself without his word, because
that is the lock above.

**Approved the same day, with two conditions, in his words:** *"so long as it does not distract too
heavily or attempt to become the only thing we're doing to remain distinct.. our graphs are the
biggest real estate.. I'd rather focus there."* The stamp is on the deadline figure as a trial, in the
quietest ink, under the axis; it spreads to the other figures only after he has seen it. **It is a
detail, not the answer.** The work he wants is the graphs themselves drawn in a language that is
Oath's, and that is where the next design pass spends its effort.
