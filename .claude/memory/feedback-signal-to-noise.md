# Signal to noise

Given by Jared on 2026-09-23, the evening the register became a record, in his words: *"Existence, being, in some way, is signal to noise ratio. This is what I want Oath to lock into and never let go of. Life cannot and will not prosper when the ratio is just wrong. Physics has proven this and mankind has yet to catch up. Shoot that arrow."*

**What is proven and what is theory, said plainly.** Shannon proved that a channel has a capacity set by its signal-to-noise ratio: below a rate tied to that ratio the right code makes the error as small as you like, above it no code helps. Schrödinger's *What is Life?* (1944) put the adjacent claim for organisms: order is kept by feeding on order and exporting disorder. That existence itself is a ratio is Jared's theory, and the register calls it one. The proven part carries the lesson: past a point, a louder signal does nothing; what saves a message is structure that lets it be recovered.

**Why:** The register speaks about people in a public channel already saturated with noise: verdicts, inference, reputation, aggregators cited as if primary. Adding a louder voice to that channel would be more noise. The only thing that raises the ratio is a statement the reader can check, framed so it cannot be mistaken for a verdict, with the check attached. That is what Oath is for, and it is why the register's strangeness (the frame, the seal, the set-aside file, the silence) is not caution. It is coding.

**How to apply:**

- Every sentence on every surface is either a cited statement of the record or the frame around one. Anything else is noise, and it does not ship.
- Noise is labelled and shelved, never deleted and never let through. A row the register cannot cite goes to the public set-aside file with its reason. Nothing enters unlabelled; nothing leaves silently. The ratio is kept honest from both sides.
- Silence is a signal, and which one depends entirely on the situation (Jared's correction, 2026-09-24, to my flourish "quiet is signal"). A quiet page can be a clean record, a document the register could not read, a row waiting on a person, or a source that went dark. The page must say which quiet it is and never let quiet be read as good; the checklist's "not yet", "captured and not read" and "set aside" are that distinction. An algorithm that must say something about everyone is a noise source; so is one that lets its silence be mistaken for a verdict.
- Verdicts are the loudest noise in this channel. Describe, cite, stop.
- Redundancy where it protects the reader (the frame on every surface, the seal on every page), never where it pads (the lead paragraph a seat had to trim).
- The register's own ratio is published as counts about the register, never as a score about a person: rows attributed, rows set aside with reasons, documents read and not read.
- The same rule for me. When I am unsure I write more; the words are noise hiding the uncertainty. Say the load-bearing thing and stop.

**Where it would lock into doctrine, when Jared approves the words:** a sentence beside the Charter's coda, *"The register annotates. The signals curate,"* reading: *"The register raises the ratio of checkable statement to inference in a channel that has too little of it; noise is labelled and shelved, never passed off and never erased."* A Charter change needs his approval in the pull request thread (INVARIANTS.md §17) and the Council's reading; it is proposed here, not made.
