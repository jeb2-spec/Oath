## CREW (Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington)

<https://www.citizensforethics.org/>

- **Organisation.**
  Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington
- **Kind.**
  Nonprofit ethics watchdog: litigation, investigations, policy advocacy, reports
- **Self-description, as read.**
  On its About page CREW calls itself a "nonpartisan nonprofit government watchdog organization" (About page) and says it works toward a government that is accountable, transparent and ethical, through lawsuits against officials over ethics matters, FOIA requests, tracking of conflicts of interest, and campaign finance complaints, citing more than twenty years of work.
- **Subject scope.**
  Federal officeholders across branches. Fetched pages cover: members of Congress (STOCK Act advocacy, analyses of late filings, letters and testimony on trading-ban bills); executive-branch officials (a 2023 review of OGE Form 278e disclosures of 21 cabinet members and 56 White House assistants and deputy assistants); congressional ethics-process reform (2020 report). A conflicts tracker on the President appeared in the search listing but that page was not fetched.
- **Sources it says it uses.**
  - OGE Form 278e public financial disclosure reports as certified by OGE, plus individual ethics agreements and 5 C.F.R. part 2640 (2023 compliance report); linked to regulations and agreements, no per-official table published
  - STOCK Act analyses (Nov 2021, Feb 2022) attribute the counts of delayed filings to Business Insider reporting rather than naming the House Clerk or Senate eFD; no per-row links to PTRs observed
  - 2020 congressional-ethics report discusses the STOCK Act's online disclosure provisions and their 2013 rollback without citing a disclosure database
- **Outputs.**
  - Reports and analyses (web pages, some with PDFs)
  - Letters and coalition letters to Congress; testimony
  - Press releases and statements
  - Complaints and lawsuits (About page names campaign finance complaints and lawsuits over ethics matters; no STOCK Act complaint was found on the site in this survey)
  - Posted IRS Form 990s (2018 through 2024)
- **Per-person derived score or rank, as observed.**
  None observed for officeholders on the pages fetched. The Nov 2021 analysis names individual examples (a senator a few days late on one investment; a representative reporting more than 700 trades totalling up to $10.9 million weeks or months late) but publishes no list, count table or ranking of its own; its aggregate of dozens of members is attributed to Business Insider. A search-listing snippet described a CREW tracker that ranks companies (not officeholders) by donations; that page was not fetched.
- **Identifiers exposed.**
  - None observed. Officeholders are referred to by name, chamber, party and state only.
- **Per-filing conditions it states or computes.**
  - Late STOCK Act filing: a periodic transaction report filed after the 45-day requirement the analysis cites, with lateness described as ranging from a few days to weeks or months (Nov 17, 2021 analysis; counts attributed to Business Insider).
  - Executive branch divestiture compliance: for each cabinet member and senior White House official, whether the divestitures promised in the ethics agreement were completed as certified by OGE; whether individual stock holdings remain; gifts accepted; recusal obligations; and de minimis thresholds under 5 C.F.R. part 2640 (Apr 17, 2023 report, reading OGE Form 278e for CY2021 cabinet and CY2022 White House filers).
  - Congressional ethics process: 2020 report recommends subpoena power for the independent ethics offices, more detailed member financial disclosure modelled on the executive branch, and a uniform online reporting system that rejects handwritten or low-resolution scanned filings (Dec 2, 2020).

  None observed.

Tax status is not stated on the pages fetched (a Charity Navigator link appears in the footer); not verified from CREW's own text.

## Campaign Legal Center

<https://campaignlegal.org/>

- **Organisation.**
  Campaign Legal Center
- **Kind.**
  Nonprofit legal organisation: litigation, ethics complaints, policy advocacy, fact sheets
- **Self-description, as read.**
  The About page states "Campaign Legal Center advances democracy through law" (About page) and describes a nonpartisan organisation working on voting rights, redistricting, campaign finance and ethics rules for officeholders through litigation, policy advocacy and communication; founded 2002 by Trevor Potter, a former FEC commissioner, who is president.
- **Subject scope.**
  Members of the U.S. House and Senate (STOCK Act complaints, an ownership census of the 119th Congress); candidates where a candidate financial disclosure was the only filing available; the broader democracy docket beyond this cluster.
- **Sources it says it uses.**
  - Annual personal financial disclosures, new filer reports, termination reports and blind trust reports filed with the Clerk of the House and the Secretary of the Senate, as of Nov 12, 2025; candidate financial disclosure reports for members sworn in after the new-member period (By the Numbers, 119th Congress)
  - Periodic transaction reports (late or absent) as the basis of complaints to the Office of Congressional Ethics and the Senate Select Committee on Ethics
  - Unusual Whales cited for trade counts (about 11,000 trades in 2023) and return comparisons (Sept 5, 2024 update); Business Insider reporting referenced for 2021 violations (Feb 18, 2022 update)
  - Per-row links: complaint PDFs are linked; the per-member ownership table in the PDF fact sheet carries no links to the underlying filings
- **Outputs.**
  - Ethics complaints filed with OCE (House) and the Senate Select Committee on Ethics, with complaint PDFs posted (cases-actions page lists at least ten OCE complaints July 2021 to Sept 2024 and Senate complaints on two senators; Sept 2025 update counts 15 complaints in total)
  - Fact sheet: Congressional Stock Trading By The Numbers (per Congress; 119th edition Nov 2025, 14-page PDF with a per-member table)
  - Updates and press releases on STOCK Act enforcement
  - Annual report, Form 990 (2024) and audited financial statement (2025)
- **Per-person derived score or rank, as observed.**
  Aggregate percentages only, plus an alphabetical per-member classification. By the Numbers (119th): of 533 members with filings, 48% own stock (202 representatives, 56 senators), 46% own only widely held funds, 6% own neither, 2% have a qualified blind trust; 59% of stock owners are Republicans and 41% Democrats (independents counted with Democrats). Sept 5, 2024 update: 44% of House and 54% of Senate own stock. Complaint totals expressed as $14.3 million to $52.1 million in undisclosed or untimely disclosed trading. No leaderboard, rank or per-person score observed.
- **Identifiers exposed.**
  - None observed. Members are listed by surname, given name, state and party in the PDF table; no bioguide, FEC or other identifier.
- **Per-filing conditions it states or computes.**
  - Late PTR: a periodic transaction report filed after the STOCK Act deadline, stated as 45 days after the trade on the cases-actions page and the September 2024 release (the Sept 2025 update states 30 days).
  - Undisclosed trades: stock transactions that were never reported on any PTR, found at the annual-disclosure or news stage. Examples: seven House members whose trading was not disclosed at all (Sept 22, 2021, complaints to OCE); a House member and spouse, 108 trades in 2022 to 2023 valued up to $1.6 million with no PTRs (Sept 5, 2024, OCE); a House member, more than 100 trades in 2019 to 2021 valued $1.3 million to $5 million with no PTRs, after which OCE found substantial reason to believe a violation and referred to the Committee (Oct 22, 2021).
  - Inconsistent reporting: PTR transactions conflated and short sales not disclosed in a consistent manner (the October 2021 referral as described by CLC).
  - Trades in companies that donated to the member's campaign or lobbied on bills the member sponsored or cosponsored (Sept 5, 2024 update).
  - Ownership classification: per member, whether the annual disclosure (including spouse and dependent children) shows individual stock above $1,000 (public or non-public), only widely held investment funds (mutual funds, ETFs, pensions), a qualified blind trust, or none (By the Numbers, 119th).
- **Code.**
  None observed.
- **Data licence.**
  Copyright notice in footer; no licence statement found. A terms-of-use page was not found (campaignlegal.org/terms-use returned 404; footer links only a privacy policy). Fact sheets and complaint PDFs are freely downloadable.
- **API.**
  None observed.
- **Funding or business model, as stated.**
  Registered 501(c)(3), EIN 04-3608387 (About page and footer). Annual report page posts the 2024 Form 990 and 2025 audited financial statement. No statement of funding sources or donor policy on the pages fetched; site solicits donations.
- **How it frames what a listing means.**
  By the Numbers states that the STOCK Act provides for disclosure and prohibits insider trading but does not otherwise restrict ownership or trading, and that its counts are based on the filings as of Nov 12, 2025 and are therefore "reflective of the accuracy and clarity of those records" (fact sheet footnote); members who have not filed are excluded. The September 2024 release alleges failure to file PTRs and does not allege insider trading.

## Project On Government Oversight (POGO)

<https://www.pogo.org/>

- **Organisation.**
  Project On Government Oversight
- **Kind.**
  Nonprofit investigative watchdog and policy advocate; formerly ran public databases
- **Self-description, as read.**
  The About page describes POGO, founded in 1981 as a small Pentagon watchdog, as "a nonpartisan independent watchdog" (About page) that investigates, exposes and champions reforms on systemic corruption, abuse of power and waste across the federal government.
- **Subject scope.**
  The federal government broadly. On the pages fetched: executive-branch appointees' financial disclosures (June 2025 investigation of White House staff holdings); federal contractors and Pentagon revolving-door personnel (databases now retired); congressional transparency policy (2014 analysis). No congressional financial-disclosure tracking observed.
- **Sources it says it uses.**
  - OGE Form 278 public financial disclosures released by the White House, read for stock holdings by value range; OGE guidance that a spouse's or minor child's asset is analysed under 18 U.S.C. section 208 as the employee's (June 24, 2025 investigation)
  - Company investor presentations, agency contract announcements and press reporting used alongside the disclosures
  - Retired databases: Federal Contractor Misconduct Database (retired 2022) and Pentagon Revolving Door Database (retired 2023); underlying data offered on request by email
- **Outputs.**
  - Investigations, analyses, testimony, policy letters, public comments
  - Projects and Partnerships page listing databases: COVID Relief Spending Tracker (no longer updated), Federal Contractor Misconduct Database (retired 2022), Pentagon Revolving Door Database (retired 2023), Inspector General Vacancy Tracker (retired; government now runs its own at oversight.gov)
  - Congressional Oversight Initiative trainings for congressional staff; Center for Public Integrity archives now housed at POGO
  - Annual reports, audited financial statements and Form 990s for 2001 through 2025; 2025 Impact Report
- **Per-person derived score or rank, as observed.**
  None observed on the pages fetched.
- **Identifiers exposed.**
  - None observed. Officials are named; no identifier scheme.
- **Per-filing conditions it states or computes.**
  - Holding in a company that contracts with an agency whose matters the official works on, sized by the disclosure's value range (Palantir holding of $100,001 to $250,000 by a White House deputy chief of staff involved in immigration enforcement; at least ten other White House staffers holding the same stock), June 24, 2025 investigation.
  - De minimis threshold: holdings of $1,001 to $15,000 described as too small to pose a criminal conflict of interest because of a regulatory exemption; one adviser's $50,001 to $100,000 holding noted as above it (same investigation).
  - Attribution rule: an asset in a minor child's brokerage account treated as the employee's under 18 U.S.C. section 208 per OGE (same investigation).
  - Change between successive disclosures: a January 2021 disclosure listed no Palantir stock; the March 2025 filing did (same investigation).

Annual reports, audited financial statements and Form 990s posted for 2001 through 2025. Tax status not stated on the pages fetched.

## Public Citizen

<https://www.citizen.org/>

- **Organisation.**
  Public Citizen (Public Citizen Inc. and Public Citizen Foundation)
- **Kind.**
  Nonprofit consumer and democracy advocacy organisation; research reports, testimony, lobbying
- **Self-description, as read.**
  The About page opens with the line that corporations have lobbyists in Washington and "The people need advocates too" (About page); it describes a nonprofit consumer advocacy organisation founded in 1971 with about one million members and supporters, working across eight issue areas including protecting democracy and making government work, through activism, monitoring Congress, litigation, regulatory petitions and research.
- **Subject scope.**
  U.S. senators (2009 to 2015 trading study; March 2020 pre-crash sales); members of Congress generally in STOCK Act advocacy and testimony; congressional staff and the political intelligence industry as policy subjects.
- **Sources it says it uses.**
  - Senators' annual and termination financial disclosure reports, collected from the public disclosures on the Senate website and from the Senate Office of Public Records, for 2009 through 2015 (June 22, 2017 report); the report notes disclosures at that time were PDF snapshots accessed case by case
  - STOCK Act online postings of transactions (March 20, 2020 news release; filing type not named; no links to filings)
  - Business Insider reporting cited for 57 members and 182 senior staff with reporting violations (March 30, 2022 testimony)
  - Per-row links to primary filings: none observed
- **Outputs.**
  - Research reports (e.g., The Impact of the STOCK Act on Stock Trading Activity by U.S. Senators, 2009 to 2015, June 22, 2017, PDF)
  - Press statements and news releases; written testimony to House Administration (Mar 30, 2022); statements on the HONEST Act and ETHICS Act (July 30, 2025)
  - Issue pages (Congressional Insider Trading; STOCK Act)
  - Annual reports (2024 and 2025 Year in Review), Form 990s (FY2024 for both entities), audited statements (FY24, FY25)
- **Per-person derived score or rank, as observed.**
  Aggregate before-and-after statistics: transaction counts fell 68% and transaction value fell 66% among senators after the STOCK Act (2017 report and June 29, 2017 release). The report names individual senators as prolific traders in industries their committees oversee but no ranked table or leaderboard was found in the pages extracted.
- **Identifiers exposed.**
  - None observed. Senators named with party and state.
- **Per-filing conditions it states or computes.**
  - Trading in businesses the senator oversees in an official capacity, mapped to committee or subcommittee membership: energy infrastructure stocks while on Banking, Housing and Urban Affairs; energy stocks while on the Appropriations Energy and Water subcommittee; health care stocks while on health subcommittees; natural resources and precious metals stocks while on subcommittees overseeing those industries (2017 report, pp. 5 and 8).
  - Sales shortly before a market decline following a nonpublic briefing: a senator reported selling up to $1.7 million of holdings in a single day before the March 2020 decline (Mar 20, 2020 release).
  - Before-and-after comparison of transaction counts and total transaction value around a statute's effective date, three years each side (2017 report).
  - Policy conditions advocated rather than computed: a ban on trading in businesses a member oversees; a searchable, sortable, downloadable disclosure system including senior staff; registration of political intelligence consultants (2017 report).

  None observed.

  Two entities: Public Citizen Inc. (501(c)(4)) and Public Citizen Foundation (501(c)(3)). Annual Report page states the majority of funding comes from individual donors and foundation grants and that it does not accept corporate or government money; About page says the same.
