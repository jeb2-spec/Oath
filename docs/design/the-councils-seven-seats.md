# The Council's seven seats

**Status:** a proposal. It amends [COUNCIL.md](../../COUNCIL.md) §3 and
[`.claude/prompts/council.md`](../../.claude/prompts/council.md), which are the Council's own
instrument. COUNCIL.md is sealed doctrine and one of the five files the meta-invariant watches, so
this lands as one sealed build carrying its amendment row, its own Council reading, and the
maintainer's approval. Nothing here is in force until that build is sealed.

## Abstract

COUNCIL.md §3 says three seats convene on every session and defines A, B and C. Seven seats have read
every pass of this project since the fourth reading of S.1b, and D, E, F and G are defined nowhere a
later reader can find them. COUNCIL.md §8 makes the committed prompt's git blob SHA the
reproducibility guarantee for a reading, so four of the seven readings that shaped this register
cannot be reproduced: a reader holding the SHA finds three seats where the findings name seven. This
note proposes the four seats in the same shape as the three, justifies each by the findings it
actually made, and proposes one extension to Seat B that the practice has already outgrown. It adds
no seat that has not already earned its place, and it proposes no change to what a seat may do.

As of the commit that adds this note, `tools/highlight-charter-change.py` refuses an amendment to the
antidrift core whose recorded reading names a seat the prompt at that reading's SHA does not define.
That gate is the reason this note is a proposal rather than a memo: the amendment is now the only way
the seven seats become citable, and the gate holds the amendment to its own standard.

## 1. Why seven, and why the four are unwritten

The three seats were written at the founding, for a project whose surfaces did not exist yet. They
are still right, and nothing here narrows them. The four that followed were not designed; they
accumulated, one per class of defect that the three seats kept missing, and each was written down in
the prose of the pass that needed it rather than in the instrument. That is how a seat becomes real
and how it stays uncitable at the same time.

The cost is not hypothetical. The second reading of the built answer produced eight blocking findings;
four of them came from seats E, F and G. Any reader who later asks *on what basis was that claim
changed?* can reach the finding, and cannot reach the seat that made it.

## 2. The four seats

Each is written the way §3 writes A, B and C: who the reader is, then what the seat watches for. Two
rules shaped the wording.

**A seat names a reader and a situation, never a surface.** A seat defined against *the answer
sentence* rots the day the answer sentence changes; a seat defined as *a person on a phone with
thirty seconds* keeps working when every page is rebuilt. Each definition below names a person; each
watch-list names classes of defect.

**A seat's justification is its catch record.** Every item under *What it has caught* is a finding
already fixed in this repository, with the pass it was made on. A seat that cannot show one has not
earned a place, and should not get one on the strength of sounding useful.

### Seat D. The Partisans

Reads twice, as two people who have already made up their minds about the officeholder the change
most affects, one on each side, each looking for the sentence they could hold up as proof the
register is against them. Watches specifically for:

- A surface whose shape differs according to what it found, which is a verdict by placement.
- A crop, a screenshot or a quoted fragment that carries a result about a person without the frame
  that makes it bearable. The unit of travel is not the page.
- A figure whose visual weight, ordering or emphasis runs one way while its numbers do not.

*What it has caught.* That the result and the frame could be cropped apart, which set the rule that
the frame lives inside the result's own paragraph (second reading of the built answer, blocking). That
a note beside a Finding saying the Clerk's copy *was a different file* read as the document having
changed under it, with Seats C and F (fifth reading of S.1b). That a count of one with the condition
written as *the officeholder* pointed at the single person the table below named, with Seat A (fifth
reading). That a correction publishes a build too, which the planned METHODOLOGY sentence denied, so a
reader could have read timing into a publication (fourth reading).

### Seat E. The Constituent the Averages Leave Out

Reads on a five-year-old phone, on a slow connection, in thirty seconds, and again with a screen
reader and at twice the text size. Watches specifically for:

- The answer the reader came for, below the fold, behind a legend, or cut mid-sentence.
- A graphic a reader needs that does not reach 3:1 against the paper, or a state told apart by colour
  or lightness alone.
- A screen reader made to say the same thing twice, or a figure whose meaning is in a visual and not
  in its caption.
- A quiet page less clear than a page where something fired.
- The practical thing at the end of a page nobody scrolls to the end of.

*What it has caught.* That the answer's paragraph ran eighty to a hundred and fifty words and fitted
no phone's first screen on any of four hundred and thirty-nine pages (second reading of the built
answer, blocking). That the square carrying the reassuring answer measured 1.64:1 against the paper
where the adverse one measured 15.69:1, so a grid of 463 read as though the adverse state were most of
it (second reading, blocking). That *read 6 of 6* followed by blame on what the register could read
was a contradiction in one paragraph (second reading, blocking). That the seal's mark, which carries
its own title and description so it can be served alone, made a screen reader say the same three
sentences twice inside its captioned figure (fifth reading). That *Verify it*, which this project's
own discipline puts at the end of every entry, sat at word 9,196 of one page and 35,816 of the
longest. That the answer is still four numbers where this reader holds two.

### Seat F. The Reader Beyond the Border

Reads the register in translation, or in a second language, and meets every sentence as a machine
renders it rather than as it was written. Watches specifically for:

- A word whose translation carries an authority the register does not have: an audit, an
  investigation, a clearance, a charge.
- The frame becoming a legal hedge, which is worse than no frame, because a hedge implies the thing
  it hedges.
- A clause whose grammar makes a property of a row read as an act of a person.
- A sentence that depends on English word order, or on a parenthesis a translation will move.

*What it has caught.* That *checked* renders as *audited*, *investigated* or *inspected for
compliance* in three of five languages, which turns a coverage fact into a clearance on a quiet page
and an adverse audit finding on a fired one (second reading of the built answer, blocking). That
*Presence in the register is not evidence of wrongdoing* renders as *not proof of offences*, which
reads abroad as a legal hedge, and that a plain companion sentence is the repair (second reading;
still the maintainer's to weigh against Vow II's own words). That *under another given name*, on a
named person's page, reads as that person having filed under one, when the row carries the name and
the person did not (fifth reading of S.1b). That *the swearing-in the roster records for the Congress*
was printed on the page of a member sworn in eleven months into it (fifth reading, live on a real
page). That dropping the sentence *it says nothing about whether this officeholder made any trade the
rule requires reported* leaves a quiet page implying more than the record holds.

### Seat G. The Reader Who Comes Later

Holds this repository and nothing else, years from now, with every URL dead and every service gone.
Watches specifically for:

- A number on a surface that is recoverable from nothing in the sealed rows.
- A sentence sourced to the code's defaults rather than to the record.
- A claim about a document, a filer or a process that no row and no run record holds.
- A definition whose words can move without its version, or a rule a build applies that the build does
  not fix.

*What it has caught.* That nine surfaces called a document the register could not read *scanned
paper*, a physical fact about 54 filed documents that no row records; the register records only that
the text it extracted carried no Filing ID line and no State/District line (fifth reading of S.1b,
raised, published anyway, and caught again on the pass that built the figure meant to honour it). That
the filing year every page names came from a Python default argument, so a build with no run record
published 1,644 sentences about a specific year's Clerk index sourced to nothing (second reading of
the built answer, blocking). That the answer's words were keyed by a Signal's slug alone, so a version
2 would publish version 1's account of the rule, which is a silent redefinition moved into the
sentence a reader meets, with Seat C (second reading, blocking). That the signal run record has no
schema while one of the answer's four numbers is recoverable from nothing else. That SUBJECTS.md is
the rule every build applies to who may appear and is not among the sealed doctrine, so a build does
not fix the rule it was built under. That EVIDENCE §7 and INVARIANTS §16 promise a reader a filing's
bytes the register does not keep.

## 3. The extension to Seat B

At the third reading of S.1b, Seat B was not asked to report. It was asked to decide: whether the
register should keep the bytes of a filed document. It chose that the register should not, because a
kept copy would outlast the Clerk's withdrawal or redaction of a private person's name written into a
report's lines, and the §14 gate would then refuse the copy's removal. The code and the pages follow
that decision today, and cite it.

COUNCIL.md §3 does not describe that. Seat B as written *reads* and *watches for*; it does not decide,
and §6 gives deciding to the maintainer. The practice outgrew the text, and an undocumented power is
the thing this project's own doctrine exists to prevent, whoever holds it.

The proposed extension, narrow on purpose:

> Where a design question turns on what a subject, or a private person named beside them, would have
> to bear, the maintainer may put that question to Seat B and be bound by its answer for that
> question alone. Seat B's answer is recorded as a Council finding of category `other` and severity
> `blocking`, with the reason, and the maintainer's ruling under §6 remains the route to override it.
> Seat B does not decide anything else, and no other seat decides anything.

It narrows rather than widens: it names the one thing that has already happened, bounds it to
questions about what a person must bear, and leaves §6 and §7 in force over it.

## 4. Threats to validity, in the order a careful reader would raise them

- **A catch record justifies a seat and cannot validate one.** Every finding listed above is real, and
  a different seven seats might have caught the same things. The record shows these seats worked; it
  does not show they are the right seven. Nothing here claims otherwise, and a seat that stops
  catching anything should be removed by the same route it arrived by.
- **The seats were written by the hand they review.** Every reading in this record was held by one AI
  session sitting seats sequentially, which is what COUNCIL.md §3 permits and §9 addresses, and it is
  the weakest part of the arrangement. Seven viewpoints from one session are not seven reviewers. What
  the arrangement does buy is that a defect must survive seven deliberately hostile readings, and the
  record shows it often does not: the missing noun in the answer's result clause was found
  independently by five seats of seven.
- **A seat's definition is prose, and prose drifts.** The gate checks that a seat a reading names is
  defined in the prompt at that reading's SHA. It cannot check that the definition still means what it
  meant. That is what the blob SHA is for: the definition a reading sat against is fetchable, whatever
  the prompt says today.
- **This note is not doctrine.** Until the sealed build lands, COUNCIL.md still says three seats
  convene, and a reading naming seven is a reading that exceeds the instrument. Saying so here is the
  honest state, not a licence.

## 5. What the amendment needs, and what it cannot take from me

The mechanical part is small: COUNCIL.md §3 gains the four seats and the extension, the prompt
gains the same four in its own register, and `tools/test_highlight_charter_change.py`'s assertion
about which seats the prompt defines is extended in the same commit.

A working draft of exactly that was cut on 2026-09-26, 220 added lines across COUNCIL.md, the prompt
and INVARIANTS.md, and it is **not durable**: it sits as uncommitted files on two local-only branches
in one cloud session's scratchpad, and it goes when that container is reclaimed. Little is lost that
this document cannot rebuild, because §2 above carries each seat's definition and watch-list whole.
What would have to be written again is the prompt's own register: the same four seats addressed to the
reader who sits in one, rather than stated as doctrine. Saying that is better than letting a later
reader read *written* and think the text is in hand.

One thing the draft settled differently from this document, and it is the maintainer's to settle. §2
above heads Seat F **The Reader Beyond the Border**; the draft heads it **The Person Beyond the
Border**, in COUNCIL.md and in the prompt alike. The draft's reading is that this seat is named for
someone the decisions reach rather than for someone reading a page, and Seat F is the one seat of the
four where those are not the same person; against it, every other seat is named as a reader. Both
names must not ship: COUNCIL.md §8 makes the prompt's blob SHA the reproducibility guarantee, and a
seat the two documents name differently is a reading nobody can reproduce.

Three things it needs that are not mine to supply.

1. **The maintainer's approval.** The amendment row's `approved_by` says who approved it. Writing
   *the maintainer* there before the maintainer has approved it would be a row asserting a decision
   nobody made, which is the exact defect the whole register is built to prevent. The row waits.
2. **Its own Council reading.** COUNCIL.md §8 makes amendments to the Council's instrument
   Council-reviewed on purpose, because a prompt that softens a seat quietly softens every review
   after it. That reading is held against the prompt as it stands, by the three seats the prompt
   defines, and its SHA goes in the row.
3. **A re-seal.** COUNCIL.md is sealed, so amending it changes the build's digest. Build
   `0005-house-2025`'s OpenTimestamps proof is **confirmed at Bitcoin block 968733** as of
   2026-09-27, which makes this stricter and not looser: a re-seal under the same build id would
   leave the ledger pointing a completed proof at a file that no longer exists, and that proof is
   the earliest evidence the register holds of its own contents. The honest form is a new
   build id carrying the same rows, its own manifest, and an anchor marked owed, with ANCHORS.md
   showing both. That changes the published integrity digest of the register, which is not a decision
   to take on one engineer's judgement.
