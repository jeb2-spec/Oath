# Periodic Transaction Report dated after the STOCK Act deadline

## Description

A Member of the House reports a purchase, sale or exchange of stocks, bonds, commodity futures or other securities over $1,000 on a Periodic Transaction Report, due within 30 days of being notified of the transaction and in no case later than 45 days after it. This Signal sets the date the Clerk's public index gives each House report the register has read beside the dates the report prints for each transaction on it that the rule plainly reaches. It fires for a report when, for at least one of those transactions, the index date is later than the deadline, and it records by how many days and for which transactions.

## Standard

[STANDARDS.md S.2](../../STANDARDS.md#s2-stop-trading-on-congressional-knowledge-stock-act-of-2012). The deadline is [5 U.S.C. § 13105(l)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title5-section13105&num=0&edition=prelim), added to the Ethics in Government Act by the STOCK Act of 2012 ([Pub. L. 112-105](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/PLAW-112publ105)): a report of a transaction is due "not later than 30 days after receiving notification of any transaction required to be reported under section 13104(a)(5)(B), but in no case later than 45 days after such transaction." The House Committee on Ethics states the same deadline as the earlier of 30 days from being made aware of the transaction or 45 days from it ([Financial Disclosure](https://ethics.house.gov/financial-disclosure)). Its [Periodic Transaction Report form and instructions for CY 2025](https://ethics.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Final-CY-2025-PTR-Form-1.pdf), which the register read on 2026-09-21 and 2026-09-23, state that the due date is not extended when it falls on a weekend or holiday; name the assets the report covers, stocks, bonds, commodities, futures and other securities, cryptocurrencies among them; exclude others, among them real property, widely held investment funds, the Thrift Savings Plan and transactions solely between the filer, the spouse and dependent children; and define the transaction date and the notification date the report prints. Its [memorandum of 30 January 2023](https://ethics.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/FINAL-PTR-Due-Date-Pink-Sheet.pdf) and a later memorandum on the same subject ([PTRPinksheet_current.pdf](https://ethics.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/PTRPinksheet_current.pdf)) state the due-date rule too; STANDARDS.md S.2 says which of these the register has read at the source and which it has only seen returned by a search.

## Inputs

- **transaction.** `transaction_date`, `notified_date` and `filing_status` as the report prints them; `asset_code`, the code the report prints for the asset, as the Clerk's [legend](https://fd.house.gov/reference/asset-type-codes.aspx) defines it; `asset`, the asset's name as printed; `amount_range`; `filing_id`.
- **filing.** `filed_at`, the date the Clerk's public index gives the report; `form_type`; `extraction_confidence`, which reads `structured` only where the register read the report and its printed Filing ID agreed with the index.
- **officeholder.** `sworn_at`, the date the Clerk's roster records the officeholder as sworn in for the Congress it lists.

## Criteria

The Signal reads every report of form type `House-PTR` in the register. A report the register did not read, which is a scanned paper filing, is not evaluated: the Committee judges a paper report by its postmark, and the register does not see the postmark. A report whose date in the Clerk's index cannot be read is not evaluated.

For every other report it takes each transaction row in turn and evaluates it only where the rule plainly reaches it. Otherwise it counts the row, with the first of these reasons that applies. The report marks the row with a filing status other than New, or with none: an amendment is filed after the report it amends, and the rule measures the original. Its transaction date is not a real calendar date written as the report writes dates. Its asset is not coded `ST` (stocks), `CS` (corporate bonds and notes), `OP` (options) or `CT` (cryptocurrency), as the Clerk's legend gives the codes, or carries no code: the Committee's instructions keep widely held funds, real property and some other assets off these reports, though a filer may list them, and the register has not read the instructions to settle where others belong, government securities among them. It is coded `ST` and its printed name calls it an ETF, a widely held fund. Its amount is $1,000 or less, which the rule does not reach. The roster records no swearing-in date for the officeholder, or the transaction is dated before the swearing-in the roster records for this Congress: the roster records that date, not the start of a person's service, and a returning Member's earlier transactions were under the same rule, but the register holds no earlier index against which to tell a transaction first reported now from one reported before, so this version does not evaluate them. The transaction is dated after the report. Its deadline, below, falls before 2025: the instructions the register has read are the CY 2025 form's, and earlier instructions have not always said the same thing about a weekend.

For a row it evaluates, the deadline is the earlier of 30 days after the notification date the report prints and 45 days after the transaction date. Where the report prints no notification date, or one that cannot be read, or one before the transaction date or after the report's own date, the 30-day limit is not applied and the deadline is 45 days after the transaction, which does not depend on it. Days are calendar days: 30 days after 13 January is 12 February. The deadline does not move when it falls on a weekend or a federal holiday, as the Committee's instructions state. Where the two limits fall on the same day, the 45-day limit is named as setting it.

A row is after the deadline when the Clerk's index dates the report later than the deadline, by the difference in days. The Signal fires for a report when at least one row it evaluated is after the deadline. The Finding names the report and the officeholder the register attributes it to. For every row after the deadline it gives the dates as printed, the deadline, the limit that set it and the days after; whether the report prints a notification date later than 45 days after the transaction, and how many days after that notification the index dates the report; and, where the deadline fell on a Saturday, a Sunday or a federal holiday as 5 U.S.C. § 6103 lists them on the days observed, the first business day after it. The sentence says so where that decides the Finding, which is where the index dates the report on or before that first business day. The Finding also counts the rows on the report the Signal evaluated and the rows it did not, by reason, and carries the frame.

    in scope(row) = marked New; coded ST, CS, OP or CT, and not an ST named as an ETF;
                    over $1,000; traded on or after this Congress's swearing-in and on or
                    before the report
    deadline(row) = min(notified + 30, traded + 45)   when traded <= notified <= report date
                  = traded + 45                       otherwise
    after(row)    = report date - deadline(row)       after when 1 or more, for a deadline
                                                      in 2025 or later

The implementation that writes the Findings is [`src/signals/stock-act-ptr-after-deadline.py`](../../src/signals/stock-act-ptr-after-deadline.py). The reference implementation, [`src/signals/stock-act-ptr-after-deadline.ts`](../../src/signals/stock-act-ptr-after-deadline.ts), shares no code with it and must agree with it on every known-answer case and every row of the register; `python tools/rebuild.py <finding-id>` regenerates any Finding from the rows it names. ## What this Signal does not say

It does not say the House Committee on Ethics found a report late, assessed a fee, waived one, or took any other action: the register sees none of the Committee's decisions. Under the Committee's instructions a fee applies to a report filed more than 30 days late, and the Committee may waive it; the register computes no fee for anyone. It does not say the officeholder meant to delay a report, knew of a transaction earlier than the report says, or gained anything from its timing. It says nothing about the transactions themselves, which the rule requires reported and does not prohibit, and nothing about who holds an asset: many rows are a spouse's, a dependent child's or held jointly, whether or not the filer marked them, and a row the filer lists under a trust, a foundation or another account is evaluated as listed.

A report may list transactions the rule does not require on it, and this Signal does not evaluate them. Nor does it evaluate reports the register has not read, rows marked Amended or Deleted or marked with no status, or transactions dated before this Congress's swearing-in, so a page on which it did not fire may still hold such a report or row, and the page says how many; an officeholder whose reports are all scanned paper cannot be among those on which it fired, whatever the reports show. A transaction reported on more than one report is evaluated on each. The Signal relies on the date the Clerk's public index gives a report and on the dates the report prints. The register has not compared the index date with the date signed on each report, or learned in which time zone the index keeps it; where a Finding turns on a day or two, its table shows how many. Where either date is wrong, a Finding repeats the error, and the correction follows the record by supersession, never by removal. It does not rank, total or compare officeholders, and a report with many rows after the deadline is one report.

## Worked example

Against [`fixtures/stock-act-ptr-after-deadline/cases.json`](../../fixtures/stock-act-ptr-after-deadline/cases.json), thirty-six cases with no person in them, each answer worked by hand. A transaction on 2 January 2025, notified on 20 January, has a deadline of 16 February, 45 days after the transaction and earlier than 30 days after the notification: a report dated 16 February is on time and one dated 17 February is after the deadline by 1 day. A report dated 10 April 2025 listing a transaction of 3 March notified the same day (deadline 2 April), one of 20 March notified the same day (deadline 19 April) and one marked Amended fires for the first row only, with this text: "The Clerk's index dates this report 2025-04-10, which is later than the deadline the rule sets for 1 of the 2 transactions on it that this Signal evaluated, by 8 days. The deadline is the earlier of 30 days after the notification date the report prints and 45 days after the transaction date (5 U.S.C. § 13105(l))." A transaction of 31 July 2025 notified the same day has a deadline of Saturday 30 August; Monday 1 September was Labor Day, so a report dated Tuesday 2 September is after the deadline by 3 days, and its sentence ends: "For that transaction the deadline fell on a Saturday, and the Clerk's index dates the report on or before the first business day after it, 2025-09-02 (Labor Day, 2025-09-01, was a federal holiday); the Committee's instructions for these reports state that a due date on a weekend or holiday does not move." A notification date printed before the transaction date is not applied, so a report of 5 May for a transaction of 10 April with a printed notification of 1 April does not fire, where applying that date would have put it 4 days after 1 May. A row coded `GS` is counted and not evaluated, whatever its dates.
